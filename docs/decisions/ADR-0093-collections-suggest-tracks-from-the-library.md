# ADR-0093: Collections Suggest Tracks From the Library

Status: proposed

Date: 2026-08-25

Extends [ADR-0048](ADR-0048-seeded-playlists-are-generated-from-analysis.md).

Implementation:
- **Rebuilt on per-seed voting 2026-08-25, after the clustering version was deployed and used.**
  The Decision above is the second mechanism; the record of the first is kept below rather than
  deleted, because the way it failed is the finding. Points 1, 4, 5, 6 and 8 were unchanged by the
  rebuild.
- Server half built and deployed to the NAS 2026-08-25. Verified against the real library: 1,730
  favourites, seeded from 600, in 1.2 s warm; deterministic across runs, no duplicate rows, and zero
  overlap with the collection. The 9-track Merck IDM playlist answers in 0.18 s with Mr. Projectile,
  Solvent, Proswell, Datassette and proem.
- **`NEIGHBOURS_PER_SEED` could not be a constant, and a fixed 10 broke the small case badly.**
  Measured: a 9-track playlist taking ten neighbours per seed produced *zero* candidates with two
  votes, so `MIN_VOTES` never engaged and every result came from the single-vote top-up the floor
  exists to suppress — a worse list than the centroid it replaced. At 30 the same playlist produced
  eight agreed candidates. `_neighbours_for` now holds the total ballot count roughly constant, so
  nine seeds cast wide and six hundred cast at the floor. The failure is worth recording because the
  mechanism looked correct and the tests passed: only the real library has a collection small enough
  to expose it.
- **The artist and album caps do not catch the same song filed twice.** "Mr. Projectile — None"
  appeared twice in one list of ten, because the two rows carry different albums and pass both caps.
  `_drop_duplicate_recordings` folds them using `normalize_for_duplicate_matching`, the same
  normalisation `duplicate_detection` uses, so the two features agree about what is the same
  recording.
- **The first live run found that `POOL_SIZE = 400` had never been 400.** pgvector's HNSW scan is
  capped by `hnsw.ef_search`, which defaults to **40** and overrides `LIMIT`; measured on the 26k
  library, `ORDER BY embedding <=> … LIMIT 400` returned exactly 40 rows. So ADR-0048's pool has
  been a tenth of its documented size since it shipped, and `ambient.get_candidates`' "top 150"
  (ADR-0005) is likewise 40. `rank_for_seed` now issues `SET LOCAL hnsw.ef_search = 500`, which
  restores the 400 at a measured 12 ms. **`ambient` is deliberately not changed** — `AMBIENT` is
  regression-locked by `tests/test_ambient_scoring.py` and its weights were tuned against whatever
  the pool actually was, so widening it there changes shipped, tuned behaviour and needs its own
  decision. Recorded here rather than in ADR-0048 because this is where it was found.
  The symptom that exposed it was `pool_size` reading 32–38 in the response — the diagnostic field
  point 2 put there, doing its job on the first real request.
- **The first mechanism was k-means clustering with a centroid per cluster, and its record is
  this: three attempts to name a cluster, all of which failed.** Tagging the centroid through
  `mood_tags` scored 0.579 for its winning descriptor against 0.505 for fourth place — confident-
  looking and meaningless — and captioned a cluster of Ford & Lopatin and Yelle "Country · Blues".
  Counting per-track `TrackAnalysis.mood_tags` and ranking them by over-representation was better
  sourced and cost no query, but still ran through a 48-word vocabulary with no word for Ochre:
  "Blues · Slow" over Plaid and Studio Pressure. Naming a cluster after the artists nearest its
  centroid gave "The Flashbulb · Éric Serra" for a cluster that is plainly Boards of Canada, Brian
  Eno and FSOL — most central is not most representative. Each attempt looked like a labelling bug
  and was actually the same modelling bug, which is why the mechanism was replaced rather than the
  labels fixed a fourth time.
- **That version's determinism needed an `ORDER BY`, not just a fixed RNG seed** — worth keeping
  because the lesson outlives the code. k-means++ seeds itself by row index and PostgreSQL returns
  rows in plan order, so the mixes were a function of the query plan: the same 600 favourites gave
  `[186, 139, 82, 193]` ordered by id, `[206, 235, 99, 60]` by title, `[87, 156, 244, 113]`
  unordered. **Comparing two consecutive responses does not catch this** — they share a plan and
  agree — which is how the endpoint passed a determinism check while being wrong.
- **`mood_tags`' warm-cache path never fired.** `mood_tags:descriptor_embeddings` is written only
  while analysis runs and carries a 24 hour TTL, so on a settled library the key is simply absent.
  The 48 vectors are a pure function of `DESCRIPTORS` and the CLAP text encoder, so they are now
  precomputed and committed (`app/services/data/mood_descriptors.npz`, 90 KB, regenerated by
  `scripts/build_mood_descriptors.py`). **Nothing in this feature reads them any more** — the fix
  is kept because it removes a 1.5 GB model load from `compute_mood_tags` during analysis, not
  because suggestions need it.

## Context

The habit Spotify built and Familiar has no answer to is the strip of recommended tracks under a
playlist: things you could add, one tap to add them. The request that prompted this asked for that,
and then named the case that makes it interesting — the same affordance for Favorites, where
"scroll to the bottom" is not a thing anyone will do.

**Two premises look wrong from the outside and are recorded here so nobody re-derives them.**

**`GET /playlists/{id}/recommendations` already exists and is a different feature.**
`RecommendationsService` (`backend/app/services/recommendations.py`) asks Last.fm for similar
artists, falls back to Bandcamp, and resolves releases through MusicBrainz. Every result is music
that is **not in the library** — `RecommendedTrack.local_track_id` is `None` for the ones that
matter, and the affordance it supports is an external link. That answers "what should I go and get".
It cannot answer "what should I add", because you cannot add a track you do not have. It is not
extended, not renamed, and not gated differently; it stays as it is.

**Most of the recommender is already built.** ADR-0048's
`backend/app/services/playlist_generation.py` is a set-seeded, in-library recommender:
`resolve_seed(track_ids=[...])` returns a centroid over the seed's CLAP embeddings plus a mean
descriptor, and `generate_seeded_playlist` pools `POOL_SIZE = 400` candidates by pgvector cosine
distance, scores them with the `PLAYLIST` profile, applies artist and album caps without discarding
the ranking (`_diverse_in_order`), and drops anything under `MIN_SCORE = 0.35`. The `PLAYLIST`
profile already weights `key` at 0.0 and `embedding` at 0.45 — ADR-0048 point 4 chose those numbers
because a set is judged as a set, not as a sequence of transitions. Pointing that pipeline at a
playlist's own tracks is nearly free.

**What it cannot do is take 1,719 seeds.** That is the size of the real Favorites collection,
verified against the live library in ADR-0012's Implementation block. `resolve_seed` builds one
centroid; the mean of 1,719 unit-ish CLAP vectors is the direction nothing in particular points, and
the tracks nearest it are the library's most generically average music. The failure is not an error
— it returns a full, plausible, useless list, which is the shape of defect this codebase keeps
finding. **So the load-bearing decision here is about the seed, not the recommender.**

One consequence of that, which is where this feature can most visibly go wrong: ADR-0048 point 5
excludes `seed.track_ids` from the result. For a whole-playlist seed that is exactly "already in the
playlist" and the right answer falls out for free. The moment the seed becomes a *sample* of a
collection, the two stop being the same set, and the endpoint starts suggesting tracks the listener
has already favourited.

## Decision

1. **A suggestion comes from the library; a recommendation comes from outside it.**
   `/recommendations` keeps its Last.fm and Bandcamp meaning. A *suggestion* is a track already in
   the library, addable in one action. The two live on the same resource and the names carry the
   difference — `suggested-tracks` rather than `suggestions`, because `POST /queue/suggestions`
   already means next-track radio and
   [ADR-0074](ADR-0074-the-queue-tag-names-three-different-things.md) is the record of what one name
   covering several things costs.

2. **One service answers for any collection.** A playlist and Favorites differ only in where the
   seed ids come from and how many there are. The pipeline is: **seed set → per-seed nearest
   neighbours → vote → exclude → diversify → one ranked list**. There is no clustering stage and no
   grouping; a small playlist is the same code path with fewer seeds.

3. **Candidates are chosen by agreement between seeds, never by their average.** Each seed track
   contributes its own `NEIGHBOURS_PER_SEED` nearest neighbours, and a candidate is ranked by how
   many seeds reached it. A mean over a multimodal collection describes none of it — that is what
   the first two attempts got wrong, at two different scales — while agreement between independent
   seeds is both the more trustworthy signal and the explainable one.

   `MIN_VOTES = 2` is not tuning. Measured on the real library, a 9-track playlist ranked by summed
   similarity alone admitted a soundtrack cue that exactly one seed had reached; on a large
   collection the same single votes are the ones a centroid would have averaged away. Requiring
   agreement removes them without needing a second mechanism for small collections. Single votes
   still fill the list when agreement cannot be reached, because a three-track playlist that
   answers nothing is worse than one that answers with less confidence.

4. **The exclusion set is the whole collection, not the group's seed.** Stated separately from point
   3 rather than left to fall out of it, because under ADR-0048 it *did* fall out and now does not.
   `rank_for_seed` takes an explicit `exclude_track_ids`, and both callers pass every id in the
   collection — not the sample, not the cluster.

5. **Suggestions preview and save nothing.** This does **not** contradict ADR-0048 point 6. That
   point refused a preview because the affordance was a context menu reading "Make a playlist", and
   a preview step would have made the button a lie. The reason was the promise, not the preview.
   "Suggestions" promises the opposite — browsing and choosing is the entire feature. Recorded
   explicitly so a later reader does not find point 6 and conclude this was forbidden.

6. **The seed is capped and the result is deterministic.** At most `SEED_SAMPLE_CAP` tracks, taken
   most-recent-first — `favorited_at` descending for Favorites, playlist position for a playlist.
   Cost then stops scaling with collection size, and k-means runs from a fixed seed so the same
   collection returns the same mixes in the same order on every request. A panel whose contents
   reshuffle each time it opens cannot be returned to.

7. **A suggestion explains itself with a real track, not a generated label.** Every suggestion
   carries the seed that reached it most strongly, so the reason is "because you like *Men I Trust —
   Show Me How*" — a real pair of tracks and a real similarity. It cannot be wrong about itself, and
   it needs no vocabulary.

   This replaces three attempts at naming a group, each of which failed, and they are listed so
   nobody retries them: tagging the cluster centroid through `mood_tags` scored 0.579 for its winner
   against 0.505 for fourth place and captioned a cluster of Ford & Lopatin and Yelle "Country ·
   Blues"; counting per-track mood tags was better sourced but still funnelled through a 48-word
   vocabulary with no word for Ochre, giving "Blues · Slow" for the Ochre/Plaid/Kettel cluster; and
   naming a cluster after its most central artists gave "The Flashbulb · Éric Serra" for a cluster
   that is plainly Boards of Canada, Brian Eno and FSOL — most central is not most representative.

   **The labels were never the disease.** A cluster produced by k=4 is an artefact of the k, and a
   centroid is a point no track occupies, so there was nothing truthful to call it.

8. **The client is a toolbar item opening a sheet, on both platforms.** Not a footer. Favorites'
   footer is unreachable — that is the request that started this — and on the Mac
   `BrowseDetailView`'s `tableBody` puts a height-filling `TrackTable` below the header, so there is
   no "below the list" to append to. One pattern serves both, and the browser gets nothing:
   [ADR-0058](ADR-0058-the-web-app-is-an-administration-tool.md) makes it a three-destination
   administration tool, and a suggestions panel is not Library state, Tools, or Server health.

## Alternatives Considered

**Extend `RecommendationsService` to cover this.** It is named for the job and already mounted on
the playlist resource. Rejected because its signal is wrong, not merely differently tuned: Last.fm
similarity is over artist co-listening, and its results are by construction things the listener does
not own. A panel offering "add this" over a list of unowned tracks has no add action. The reason
the two look interchangeable is the word, which is why point 1 spends a name on it.

**One centroid over the whole of Favorites.** The simplest thing that could work, and it is what
`resolve_seed(track_ids=...)` does today with no changes at all. Rejected on the arithmetic: the
mean of 1,719 vectors spanning a whole taste is close to the library's own mean, so the nearest
candidates are its most average tracks. It fails by returning a plausible list, so it would have
shipped and then been quietly unused.

**Seed from the most recent 25 favourites only.** Cheap, needs no clustering, and tracks current
intent. Rejected because it discards 98% of the collection and goes stale: a listener who has not
favourited anything for a month gets a month-old panel, and a listener who just favourited one album
gets suggestions for that album under a heading that says Favorites. Point 6's cap keeps the useful
half of this idea — bounded cost — without letting recency be the only signal.

**Reuse `POST /playlists/generate` and discard the saved playlist.** No new ranking code at all.
Rejected because ADR-0048 point 6 means that endpoint has no preview mode by decision: every call
writes an `is_auto_generated` playlist. Opening a suggestions sheet would litter the sidebar with
one playlist per open, and deleting them behind the listener's back is worse than the litter.

**Use `RADIO` weights, since radio is the nearby "what next" engine.** Rejected for the reason
ADR-0048 point 4 already gives: `RADIO` scores transitions — key compatibility, BPM delta — and a
suggestion is judged against a set, not against a track it follows. Reusing it because it is nearby
is how a feature ends up subtly wrong in a way nobody can name.

**k-means clustering, each cluster seeded from its centroid.** Not a paper alternative — this was
built, deployed and measured, and is what the first version of this ADR decided. It fixed the
whole-Favorites centroid by shrinking the mean rather than removing it, and the same failure came
back one level down: a cluster of 200 tracks still has a centroid no track occupies. Every attempt
to name one failed (point 7), which was the symptom; the cause is that a k=4 grouping is an artefact
of the k rather than a fact about the collection. Per-seed voting needs no mean at all, ran faster
on the real library (0.9 s against 2.7 s over 300 seeds), and carries its own explanation.

**Add `scikit-learn` for k-means.** Moot now that nothing clusters, and recorded because it would
have been the obvious next step: rejected as a heavyweight dependency for one 30-line algorithm on a
matrix of at most 600 × 512. `numpy` is already a dependency, and
`offline_manifest._cosine_shortlist` is the existing precedent for doing embedding maths there.

**Lead with the behavioural signal — "you play this a lot but never favourited it".** The strongest
per-track evidence available, and it needs no embeddings. Rejected on density, measured on the real
library: of 26,422 active tracks only 2,718 have any play history, 333 have been played five or more
times, and **39** are played 5+ and not favourited. That is a finite cleanup list which empties as
the listener acts on it, not a surface that can answer every time it is opened. It is a real feature
and it is recorded as a follow-up rather than folded in here.

**Boost candidates by the existing taste weighting.** `rank_for_seed` already does this for seeded
playlists at `taste_weight = 0.25`. Rejected because `_fetch_taste_scores` hard-codes the
`rediscover` preset, whose `favorites_boost` is **1.0** — the favourite flag contributes nothing, so
the term reduces to "played a lot, but not lately". That is backwards for something the listener has
not added yet, and inert anyway across the 90% of the library that has never been played. The
negative half of the signal is kept, because a track skipped repeatedly is evidence either way.

## Consequences

- **Positive.** The whole selection is one statement. Clustering, centroids, a fixed RNG seed, the
  row-ordering that seed depended on, and every generated label are gone — along with the four ways
  they could each be subtly wrong.
- **Positive.** Every suggestion is explainable without extra work, because the explanation is a
  by-product of how it was chosen rather than something reconstructed afterwards.
- **Positive.** Both operations are GET and write nothing, so they can be exercised against the real
  library without the undo dance a write path needs.
- **Tradeoff.** Point 6's cap means a listener with 5,000 favourites is seeded from 600 of them, so
  a genuinely old corner of the collection can go unrepresented. The alternative is a request whose
  cost grows with the collection, on a surface opened casually.
- **Tradeoff.** One index probe per seed rather than one per collection — up to `SEED_SAMPLE_CAP` of
  them. They run inside a single lateral join, measured at 0.9 s for 300 seeds against the 2.7 s the
  clustering version took, so this is cheaper in practice; but the cost is linear in seeds where the
  centroid's was constant.
- **Tradeoff.** `MIN_VOTES` trades reach for confidence. A track that genuinely belongs but sits
  near only one of your favourites is demoted below the top-up line, and on a very small collection
  the floor cannot bind at all.
- **Positive, and unrelated to this feature.** Two bugs found while verifying it are fixed here
  because they were found here, and both are recorded as independent: `SET LOCAL hnsw.ef_search`
  restores `POOL_SIZE` for seeded playlists, and the committed descriptor vectors remove a 1.5 GB
  model load behind a dead 24-hour Redis TTL from `compute_mood_tags`. **Nothing in this feature
  reads the descriptor vectors any more** — they serve analysis, not suggestions.
- **Follow-up.** `ambient.get_candidates` and `GET /tracks/{id}/similar` are still capped at 40 by
  the same `ef_search` default. Both want widening, and both change tuned or shipped behaviour, so
  they need a decision of their own rather than riding along here.
- **Follow-up.** "You play these but have not favourited them" — 247 tracks at 2+ plays, 39 at 5+.
  Nothing today answers it: `library_discover`'s `deep_cuts` is *least*-played, `unheard_tracks`
  ignores favourites, and `smart_playlists` has no favourite field at all.
- **Follow-up.** The Apple client surface — toolbar item, sheet, and the add action wiring into the
  existing `playlistsAddTracksToPlaylist` — is a separate plan in `familiar-apple`, after the
  endpoints are proven against the real library.
- **Follow-up.** `PlaylistSeed` in `FamiliarKit` deliberately omits `track_ids` "because no native
  surface can produce one". A suggestions sheet offering "make a playlist from these" would be the
  first native surface that could. That is a decision to record, not to slide in.
